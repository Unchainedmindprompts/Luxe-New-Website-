The short answer: Alta is not a Hunter Douglas knockoff or a competitor borrowing the name. [Alta Window Fashions](https://www.altawindowfashions.com) is part of the Hunter Douglas family of brands, sold through dealers only, custom-made for your windows, and backed by a limited lifetime warranty, at prices below comparable Hunter Douglas products. What you give up is not build quality or warranty backing. What you give up is the premium layer: the signature Hunter Douglas designs, the deepest fabric libraries, and the finest finishes. I sell and install both brands, and the way most of my whole-home clients actually do it is both at once. Hunter Douglas goes in the living spaces the home shows off. Alta goes in the guest rooms, kids' rooms, and secondary spaces. Same dealer, same measuring, same installation standard, same corporate family standing behind the warranties.

## What Alta actually is

Alta Window Fashions is part of Custom Brands Group, a division of Hunter Douglas. That relationship matters more than any single product feature, because it answers the question every buyer is really asking: who stands behind this if something goes wrong in ten years? With Alta, the answer is the same parent company that stands behind Hunter Douglas.

The Alta lineup covers the workhorse categories of a home: honeycomb shades, roller shades, sheer shadings, banded shades, natural woven shades, wood and faux wood blinds, and shutters. These are custom products, measured for your windows and built to order, sold the same way Hunter Douglas is sold, through a dealer who comes to your home. Alta is not a stock product pulled off a shelf, and it is not sold in a box store aisle.

## What you keep when you choose Alta

**The warranty structure.** Alta backs its products with a [limited lifetime warranty](https://www.altawindowfashions.com) to the original purchaser, covering defects in materials, mechanisms, and workmanship. Motorization components are covered for five years. That is the same shape as the Hunter Douglas warranty: lifetime backing on the product, five years on the motorization. The two documents are not identical in every line, and the honest differences are listed below, but no buyer should choose between these brands fearing that Alta leaves them unprotected. It does not.

**The build.** Alta products are custom-made to your measurements with the fit and finish that requires. In my experience selling both brands for years, the day-to-day build quality is why I am comfortable putting Alta in a home I have put Hunter Douglas in. The difference between the brands is not whether the shade is well made. It is how much design range sits on top of that build.

**Motorization that plays with Hunter Douglas.** Alta's motorization is called BLISS Automation, and the current version, [BLISS Automation 2.0](https://blindsmax-spaces.nyc3.digitaloceanspaces.com/product-documents/Alta%20Bliss%202.0%20Automation%20Product%20Specifications%20Guide.pdf), is documented by Alta as compatible with PowerView Gen 3. With an Alta Bridge and a PowerView Gateway in the home, Alta shades can be controlled in the same PowerView app as your Hunter Douglas shades, as long as at least one PowerView shade is installed. For a mixed home, that is the detail that makes the whole strategy work: two brands, one app, one set of scenes and schedules.

**The installation standard.** At Luxe, Alta is measured and installed through the same process as Hunter Douglas, including our partnership with Hunter Douglas Installation Services, and every installation carries our lifetime installation guarantee. The product choice changes. The standard of work does not.

## What you give up

**The signature designs.** Silhouette, Pirouette, Duette, and Vignette are Hunter Douglas products, and there is no Alta version of them. Alta makes its own [sheer shadings](https://www.altawindowfashions.ca/product/sheer-shadings), honeycomb shades, and roller shades, and they are good products. They are not Silhouette. If the room deserves the signature product, and many living rooms do, that room gets Hunter Douglas. Our [Silhouette and Pirouette guide](/blog/hunter-douglas-silhouette-vs-pirouette) shows what that premium layer buys.

**Fabric and finish depth.** Hunter Douglas libraries run deeper: more fabrics, more colors, more premium finishes, more design options per product. Alta's curated selection covers the ground most homes need, and in a guest room that ground is plenty. In the great room you designed around a view and a specific look, the deeper library earns its price.

**A few warranty details.** Alta's warranty chart covers shutters for 25 years rather than the lifetime term on Hunter Douglas shutters, and operational cords for 7 years. Everything else a homeowner is likely to care about, the product itself and its motorization, carries the same headline coverage as Hunter Douglas: lifetime and five years respectively.

## The whole-home strategy

Here is how I walk a client through a house, because almost nobody's right answer is one brand everywhere.

The living room, the great room, the dining room, the primary suite: these are the rooms you live in and the rooms your home shows. They get Hunter Douglas, the signature products, the fabrics you fell for, PowerView where it earns its keep. The guest room that hosts visitors six nights a year, the kids' rooms that will be redecorated in five years, the laundry room, the home office, the bathroom that needs a solid faux wood blind: these rooms need quality, privacy, and light control, and they do not need a premium fabric library to deliver it. They get Alta.

The arithmetic is what makes clients smile. The money saved across four or five secondary rooms is real money, and it is usually the money that puts the signature shade, or PowerView, in the great room where everyone actually sees it. You end with a home that feels premium where it counts and sensible where it does not, instead of a home where the budget got spread evenly and thinly across every window regardless of the room's job.

And because both brands live under the Hunter Douglas roof, the strategy stays simple to own. One dealer who knows both lines, one measuring standard, warranties backed by the same family, and if the home is motorized, one app running both brands.

## What Alta costs

Neither brand publishes prices, and both are quoted after measuring, because window size and fabric move the number more than anything else. What I can tell you from selling both: for a comparable product, a honeycomb shade against a honeycomb shade, a roller shade against a roller shade, Alta lands meaningfully below Hunter Douglas, and the gap grows with the number of windows. Our [Hunter Douglas cost guide](/blog/how-much-do-hunter-douglas-window-treatments-cost) publishes the dealer ranges for the Hunter Douglas side, product by product. Alta is quoted in the same consultation, on the same visit, so the comparison for your actual windows is a real number, not a guess from the internet.
