<h2>What Is Woodlore Plus and How Is It Built?</h2>

<p>Woodlore® Plus is a wood-composite hybrid shutter manufactured by <a href="https://normanusa.com/product/woodlore-plus-shutters/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Norman® Window Fashions</a>, a company <a href="https://normanusa.com/blog/norman-50th-anniversary/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">founded in 1974</a>. It is not solid wood, and it is not basic vinyl or foamed PVC. Its ABS louvers and wood-composite panel construction offer a different material choice from hardwood shutters.</p>

<h3>ABS Louvers</h3>

<p>Norman identifies the louvers as ABS — acrylonitrile butadiene styrene — an impact-resistant polymer also used in automobile parts and kitchen appliances. The <a href="https://normanusa.com/product/woodlore-plus-shutters/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">manufacturer's product details</a> describe engineered lighter-weight louvers and a waterproof option for locations with prolonged water exposure. Select that option where needed rather than assuming every configuration is waterproof.</p>

<h3>Engineered Louver Design</h3>

<p>Norman describes Woodlore® Plus as combining Woodlore panels and frames with engineered lighter-weight louvers. That construction allows panels up to 36 inches wide. The published specifications support those design features; they do not establish that a shutter can never sag or warp.</p>

<h3>Wood-Composite Panels and Frames</h3>

<p>The stiles are the vertical sides of the shutter panel. Woodlore® Plus combines its wood-composite panels and frames with ABS louvers. Panel dimensions, installation, and exposure still matter, so I confirm the configuration and current manufacturer specifications before ordering.</p>

<h3>Panels and Customization</h3>

<p>Panels are available up to 36 inches wide, with 1⅞″, 2½″, 3″, 3½″, and 4½″ louver options. Norman offers custom colors and specialty shapes. The available dimensions and options depend on the window and configuration, which we confirm when measuring.</p>

<h2>Why Composite Beats Solid Wood in North Idaho</h2>

<p>I get the appeal of solid wood. It is the traditional choice and it looks beautiful on installation day. But our climate is genuinely hard on natural materials, and the homes I service face three specific stresses that compound on wood shutters faster than most homeowners expect.</p>

<p><strong>Seasonal moisture changes.</strong> Room humidity, condensation, and care are more useful checks for an interior shutter than outdoor temperature alone. I look at the conditions at each window before recommending a material.</p>

<p><strong>Humidity and water exposure.</strong> For lakefront rooms, bathrooms, and kitchens, we check for condensation or direct water exposure. Norman offers a waterproof Woodlore® Plus option where prolonged water exposure is possible; the standard configuration should not be treated as waterproof.</p>

<p><strong>West and south summer sun.</strong> Strong afternoon sunlight makes light control and finish care important. We review the room's exposure and the manufacturer's finish and warranty requirements rather than assuming a material is immune to fading.</p>

<p>In 24 years across the Pacific Northwest, I've seen wood shutters change with exposure and care. Woodlore® Plus is designed for durability, but it is not a promise of unchanged appearance for the life of the home. <a href="https://normanusa.com/warranties/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Norman's limited lifetime warranty</a> covers defects in materials or workmanship for the original purchaser, subject to exclusions including normal wear, excessive humidity, and fading. Motorized components have separate coverage.</p>

<h2>What to Know About Shutters and Energy Savings</h2>

<p>The <a href="https://www.energy.gov/articles/5-tips-energy-saving-renters" rel="noopener" target="_blank">U.S. Department of Energy recommends using window coverings</a> to block direct summer sunlight and retain warmth in winter. That is general guidance about coverings, not a measured energy rating or savings guarantee for Woodlore® Plus.</p>

<p>For your home, the result depends on the existing glazing, window orientation, fit, climate, and when the louvers are open or closed. Closing coverings against unwanted summer sun and using them appropriately in winter can help manage comfort. Shutters do not replace weatherstripping, air sealing, or efficient glazing, and I would not promise a specific bill reduction without product-specific performance data.</p>

<h2>Traditional Tilt vs. InvisibleTilt — Which to Choose</h2>

<p>Traditional tilt and InvisibleTilt® offer different looks. Choose based on appearance, operation, and the options available for your configuration.</p>

<p><strong>Traditional tilt rod</strong> uses the classic visible bar that links the louvers. Choose this if you want the traditional plantation-shutter look or prefer operating a physical bar.</p>

<p><strong>InvisibleTilt®</strong> uses a hidden, integrated gear system, so the louvers operate as a synchronized unit with no visible hardware. It is the cleaner contemporary look and it preserves more of your view when louvers are open. I specify this on roughly 70% of installs now — particularly for homes with lake or mountain views where any visual interruption matters.</p>

<h2>Motorization: PerfectTilt G4</h2>

<p>Woodlore® Plus can be motorized with Norman's <strong>PerfectTilt™ G4</strong>, which the manufacturer markets as award-winning motorization. The <a href="https://normanusa.com/product/woodlore-plus-shutters/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">manufacturer lists remote and iPhone/iPad control, automated schedules, and an integrated solar panel to extend time between charges</a>. You can set schedules — close the west-facing panels at 2:30 p.m. in July, open the south-facing panels at 7 a.m. in January for solar gain — without thinking about it.</p>

<p>Motorization is most valuable in two situations: hard-to-reach windows (high clerestories, tall picture windows above stairwells) and large window arrays where manual operation becomes a daily chore. If you want the full breakdown of motorization options across our product lines, see our <a href="/products/motorization">motorization page</a>.</p>

<h2>Health and Indoor Air Quality</h2>

<p>Ask for the current emissions documentation for the exact product you are ordering. <a href="https://www.epa.gov/formaldehyde/frequent-questions-consumers-about-formaldehyde-standards-composite-wood-products-act" rel="noopener" target="_blank">EPA explains that TSCA Title VI and CARB Phase 2 set formaldehyde emission limits for regulated composite wood products</a>. Those limits address formaldehyde; they are not a blanket certification that a finished shutter is VOC-free or suitable for every person with respiratory sensitivities. Review product-specific information before making an indoor-air-quality decision.</p>

<h2>Common Questions About Woodlore® Plus Shutters</h2>

<h3>Will these shutters sag or warp over time?</h3>

<p>Woodlore® Plus is designed for durability, but no shutter should be described as unable to sag or warp under every condition. Correct sizing, installation, exposure, and care matter. Review Norman's warranty and choose the waterproof option where prolonged water exposure is expected.</p>

<h3>How easy are these to keep clean?</h3>

<p>Routine care is straightforward, but follow Norman's cleaning instructions for the exact finish and configuration. Avoid soaking a standard wood-composite panel; a waterproof option is available for locations with prolonged water exposure.</p>

<h3>Will I lose my view when the louvers are open?</h3>

<p>Not meaningfully. Panels are available up to 36 inches wide and stack tightly when fully open, preserving most of the glass area. With InvisibleTilt® you get an extra visual benefit — no center bar interrupting the view.</p>

<h3>Can these be customized for odd window shapes or sizes?</h3>

<p>Yes. Norman offers specialty shapes and custom color options. Available sizes and configurations have limits, so we confirm the measurements and manufacturer specifications for arches, angles, and other unusual openings before ordering.</p>
