Very few. And it depends entirely on what "better" means.

If better means better fabric, better motorization, better integration with the rest of your home, and a better finished product, there are very few brands that beat Hunter Douglas, and the price only goes up from there. If better means cheaper, then yes, plenty of brands sell similar-looking products for less money. Similar is not better. Are they better? Absolutely not.

## Better at what? The four tests

Fabric. Hunter Douglas publishes its [fabric and color counts](https://www.hunterdouglas.com/window-treatments/shades) line by line, and no mainstream competitor publishes numbers like them: 150 or more choices on Silhouette sheer shadings, 150 or more on Duette honeycomb shades, 370 or more on Designer Roller Shades, 450 or more on Skyline panel track blinds. Fabric is not a detail on a window treatment. Fabric is most of what you see, and most of what the light does when it comes through.

Signature products. Silhouette, Pirouette, Duette, Vignette, and Luminette are Hunter Douglas products. No other brand sells a shade under those names, and for some of them there is no true equivalent anywhere. Pirouette's contoured fabric vanes that open to a view have no analogue in the Graber line. When a homeowner falls in love with a Silhouette in a showroom or a friend's house, there is exactly one company that makes it.

Motorization and integration. [PowerView Gen 3 works with](https://help.hunterdouglas.com/hc/en-us/articles/39343059678868-Connect-PowerView-Gen-3-to-Smart-Home-Devices) Amazon Alexa, Apple Home, Google Home, Brilliant, Control4, Crestron, Elan, Josh.ai, RTI, URC, and Savant, per Hunter Douglas's own compatibility list. That breadth matters because a shade does not live alone. It lives inside a home full of other systems, and the thoughtful part of the Hunter Douglas line is how the products work together: one remote, one app, one automation platform across sheer shadings, honeycombs, rollers, and shutters.

Finish and backing. Hunter Douglas shades carry a [Lifetime Limited Warranty](https://www.hunterdouglas.com/warranty) covering defects in materials and workmanship, internal mechanisms, brackets, and components for the original purchaser, with operating cords covered for seven years and motorized components for five. A window treatment is something you operate every day for decades. The finish of the product and the company standing behind it are part of what you bought.

## The names above Hunter Douglas

Honesty requires this section. There are names that exceed Hunter Douglas in a narrow lane. Every one of them costs more, and none of them offers the breadth.

[Lutron Palladiom](https://www.lutron.com/us/en/window-treatments/shades/palladiom-roller-shades) is the name specified for architectural glass in very high-end homes: an exposed roller shade whose brackets are milled and hand-finished in solid aluminum and brass, built around a [carbon fiber tube](https://www.waverley.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/Lutron-Palladiom-Shading-System-Brochure.pdf) barely two inches in diameter that Lutron's brochure says can handle a 12 by 12 foot opening. It is a beautiful piece of engineering. It is also priced like one. Lutron publishes no prices; dealers report roughly $2,000 to $4,500 per opening for motorized Palladiom rollers, and $3,500 to $6,500 with blackout side channels. In my experience the largest openings run $4,000 a shade and up.

[CONRAD](https://conradshades.com) is the name for handwoven woven woods. The San Francisco company has spent [more than sixty-five years](https://conradshades.com/contact/careers/) weaving shades by hand from natural grasses, reeds, and fibers, sold through designers, architects, and fine window covering stores, under its own stated mission "to craft the finest window coverings available." A CONRAD shade is a piece of craft. It also prices well above Hunter Douglas's Provenance woven wood line in my experience, and CONRAD publishes no prices for a buyer to check that against, which tells you something about the tier it lives in.

Custom draperies and Roman shades from a good workroom belong in this tier too: beautiful, genuinely custom, and more expensive than the Hunter Douglas equivalents, without the product selection or the integration across a whole house.

Notice what this tier is not. It is not a bigger catalog at a better price. It is a short list of specialists, each narrower than Hunter Douglas, each charging a premium over it.

## The tall-window truth

Buyers with walls of glass ask this question in a specific form: what covers a 15 to 17 foot window?

The honest answer is that you have left standard product lines behind, in every brand. Hunter Douglas's own [specification sheet](https://cdn.website-editor.net/c118d8b14a3b418cbe79fdf748f301f2/files/uploaded/hunterdouglas-silhouette-nantucket-horizontalsheershades-brochure-specs-sheet-2020.pdf) caps Silhouette at 136 inches tall in its largest Grandiose configuration, about 11 feet. Lutron's published maximum for wired Palladiom is 144 inches wide by 168 inches tall, and 168 inches is 14 feet. A 15 to 17 foot opening exceeds both. Work at that height is specialty engineering and custom fabrication, whoever does it, and anyone who quotes you a standard product for it has not measured your window.

This is worth knowing because it reframes the whole question. At the extreme end, "which brand is better" stops being the question. The question becomes who measures honestly and tells you the truth about what your window actually requires.

## The cheaper shelf, honestly

Are there less expensive products with similar offerings for less money? Absolutely. [Graber sells](https://www.graberblinds.com/window-treatments/product-advisor/) cellular shades, roller and solar shades, sheer and layered shades, natural shades, Romans, blinds, shutters, and draperies, and grades its own lines from one to four dollar signs in its buying guide. [Bali](https://baliblinds.costco.com), Graber's sister brand, sells cellular, roller, and solar shades configured for self-measure and self-install. Store brands and online sellers fill the shelf below that. These are real products with real categories that overlap Hunter Douglas's categories.

Overlap is where the resemblance ends. What the lower shelf does not buy is the signature products, the fabric depth, the integration list, or the warranty structure described above. And some of the names people assume are cheaper Hunter Douglas alternatives are actually Hunter Douglas: Alta is Hunter Douglas's own value line, which is why a good dealer can put you in the Hunter Douglas family at more than one price point; our [Alta versus Hunter Douglas comparison](/blog/alta-vs-hunter-douglas) walks through where each line fits. (One brand deserves a separate note: [Norman](https://normanusa.com) makes excellent shutters, which is why Luxe carries Norman Woodlore shutters. A brand can be the right answer in one category and not the answer in another. That is what a dealer is for.)

## The car test

Here is how I explain it in a home.

You can buy a Honda or a Toyota. Great cars. Does a Honda give you the luxury feel, the style, and the functionality of a Mercedes, a BMW, or a Tesla? Absolutely not. Does it get you from point A to point B? Of course it does. But that was never the point, and it is the same with high-end window treatments.

You spent real money on a beautiful home. The right window treatments are the thing that makes the place stop people in the doorway. And it is more than the look. It is how the product filters light into the space, which is where the second wow lives, the one that is both style and quality at the same time. You cannot fake that with cheap window treatments. The light tells on them every morning.

If you have read this far, you are probably not shopping for the cheapest way to cover glass. Your other purchases likely hold the same standard. So yes: on fabric, on motorization, on integration, on finish, and most importantly on the customer experience behind the product, Hunter Douglas blinds are better. I have been doing this since 2002, and there is not a single commercial brand I would recommend over Hunter Douglas on quality of product, selection, finished look, and the experience when something needs service. That last part is what matters when the boxes are gone and you are living with the product, because that is where a window treatment either earns its price or does not.
