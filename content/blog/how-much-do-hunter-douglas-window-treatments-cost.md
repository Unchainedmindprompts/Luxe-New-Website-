Start with the honest answer: most [Hunter Douglas](https://www.hunterdouglas.com) window treatments land between about $300 and $1,200 per window, and the spread is real, not salesmanship. Hunter Douglas does not publish prices and does not sell direct. Every product is custom-made to order and sold through dealers, so there is no sticker price to look up. What exists are the ranges dealers and pricing guides publish, and those are what this article uses. Treat every number below as a planning range for a standard window, not a quote. The quote comes after measuring, and this article explains why that is not a dodge.

| Product | Published range per window |
|---|---|
| Duette honeycomb shades | $300 to $800 |
| Silhouette window shadings | $500 to $1,400 depending on size and fabric |
| Pirouette window shadings | $500 to $1,400 depending on size and fabric |
| Designer roller and screen shades | From about $300 |
| Shutters (Heritance, NewStyle, Palm Beach) | Priced per window by size, material, and panel configuration; hardwood Heritance sits at the top of the range |
| PowerView motorization | Adds roughly $300 to $600 per window to most shades |

## Why the ranges are so wide

Five things move a Hunter Douglas quote, in roughly this order.

**Window size.** This is the big one, and the ranges above assume a standard window. A large window is a different product in material, hardware, and handling. One Portland dealer publishes its installed Silhouette pricing for a 60 by 72 inch window at $2,400 to $3,900 per window. Same product line as a small bedroom Silhouette, several times the price, because there is several times as much of it.

**Fabric tier.** Every Hunter Douglas line has fabric collections at several price levels. The entry fabric and the premium fabric in the same product can differ by hundreds of dollars per window, and features like [Duette Architella](https://www.hunterdouglas.com/window-treatments/shades/cellular-shades/duette) cell-in-cell construction or LightLock blackout channels sit at the top of their lines.

**Operating system.** A cordless manual shade is the baseline. [PowerView motorization](https://www.hunterdouglas.com/powerview) adds roughly $300 to $600 per window, and fully motorized shades commonly land between $500 and $1,200 per window all-in at standard sizes, more in premium fabrics and large sizes. Our [PowerView guide](/blog/why-we-love-hunter-douglas-powerview-motorized-shades) covers what that money buys, and the [motorized shade cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-battery-operated-motorized-shades-installation-cost-in-post-falls-coeur-dalene-id) covers battery motorization pricing on entry lines.

**Specialty shapes and tall windows.** Arches, angles, trapezoids, two-story great room glass, and sliding door applications all carry specialty hardware and labor. Published benchmarks for large and custom-shape work start around $1,500 per opening and go up from there.

**The dealer.** Hunter Douglas sets no retail price, so two dealers can quote the same shade differently, and periodic manufacturer promotions move numbers again. Which brings up the part of pricing nobody explains.

## Why the same shade gets different quotes

Part of what you are paying for is the dealer's business model. A showroom dealer carries rent, a sales staff, and an installation crew, and those costs are in every quote. Luxe Window Works is shop-at-home only. What Luxe does not carry is showroom overhead, and that difference shows up in the quote.

## What a room actually costs

Per-window ranges are hard to budget against, so here is the arithmetic on a typical room, using the ranges above. A primary bedroom with four standard windows in Duette, manual operation, mid-tier fabric: roughly $1,200 to $3,200 for the room. Add PowerView to the same four windows and the room lands around $2,400 to $5,600. A great room with five large windows is a different budget entirely, because large glass prices like the Portland example, not like the standard-window table. That gap between a bedroom and a great room is exactly why dealers measure before they quote, and why any single average price for Hunter Douglas is meaningless without the window sizes behind it.
